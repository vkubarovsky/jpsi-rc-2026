\section{The final formula is missing from the NLO paper}

The NLO paper gives $\delta_{self}$~(8), $\delta_{vert}$~(12),
$\delta_{soft}$~(13), $\delta_{hard}$ only as an unevaluated integral~(14), and
the exact Bardin--Shumeiko result~(28). \textbf{There is no compact closed form
for the total ultrarelativistic correction} --- the quantity an experiment
actually applies. The right one is Eq.~(24)/(26) of Ehlotzky \& Mitter with the
missing term restored:
\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\;
\delta(m_V,\omega_{cut})=-\frac{4\alpha}{\pi}
\left[
\left(\frac12-\ln\frac{m_V}{m_\ell}\right)\left(\ln r+\frac13\right)
+\frac12\left(L_2(r)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)
+\frac{17}{72}+R(r)
+\underbrace{\frac{r^{2}}{16}-\frac{3}{8}r}_{\text{missing from [10]}}
\right]\;}
\end{equation*}
\begin{align*}
r&=\frac{2\omega_{cut}}{m_V}, &
R(r)&=\left(r-\frac{r^{2}}{4}-\frac34\right)\left[\ln\!\left(\frac{m_V}{m_\ell}\sqrt{1-r}\right)-\frac12\right], &
L_2(x)&=-\int_0^x\frac{\ln(1-t)}{t}dt
\end{align*}
For muons add the electron-loop vacuum polarization $=\delta_{self}$ of Eq.~(8)
with $m_e$. Equivalently the missing piece is $+(\alpha/\pi)(3r/2-r^{2}/4)$ added
to $\delta$; it vanishes as $r\to0$ and reaches $5\alpha/4\pi=0.0029$ at $r=1$.

\textbf{Status of this formula.} It is \emph{not} exact in the lepton mass, and
not the $m_\ell=0$ limit either: the mass is kept in the logarithms, while terms
of relative order $(m_\ell/E)^{2}$, $E=m_V/2$, are dropped. Ehlotzky \& Mitter say
so above their Eq.~(22) (``neglecting terms of order $(m/E)^{2}$''). Numerically
$(m_e/E)^{2}=1.1\times10^{-7}$ and $(m_\mu/E)^{2}=4.7\times10^{-3}$, so at
$r_{max}=1$ it is off by $1\times10^{-7}$ for electrons and $-7\times10^{-5}$ for
muons, against the exact Eq.~(28).

\subsection*{Where it should go: Section~IV}

The formula belongs in Section~IV of the NLO draft, the ultrarelativistic
section, immediately after their Eq.~(14) --- the closed form \emph{is} the
evaluation of that integral combined with Eqs.~(8), (12) and~(13). Three
arguments, all from their own text:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item Section~IV opens by promising ``compact expressions for all contributions
      to decay width: both for virtual and real bremstrahlung ones.'' Compact
      expressions follow for every individual contribution --- but not for the
      sum, which is the only physical combination.
\item Their Eq.~(16), $\delta_{FSIC}=3\alpha/4\pi$, becomes an immediate
      corollary: it is the $r_{max}\to1$ limit of the closed form, less
      $\delta_{self}$. At present it arrives as a separate assertion.
\item Their Figure~4 plots exactly this quantity for both channels. The reader is
      shown the curve but never given the function that draws it.
\end{itemize}

Section~IV also states that the URA is ``applicable for $\ell=e$'' and that the
mass-dependent treatment is ``crucial for \ldots the muon channel''. That matches
the accuracy table above. So the electron expression should be presented as the
working formula of Section~IV, and the muon expression alongside it with the
$(m_\mu/E)^2$ error flagged and Eq.~(28) named as the fallback. Dropping the muon
formula in unqualified would contradict a sentence they have already written two
pages earlier.

This suggestion, with the formulas written out for both channels, is Section~5 of
\texttt{NLO\_comments.pdf}.

\section{The missing term in Ehlotzky's paper}

Eqs.~(14), (15), (19), (20) of the note were \emph{faithful transcriptions} of
Ehlotzky \& Mitter (E\&M) Eqs.~(24) and~(26); they now carry the restored term
(Section~8). The omission originates in 1968:
\begin{center}
VPK (11)$\leftrightarrow$E\&M (22)\quad
VPK (13)$\leftrightarrow$E\&M (23)\quad
VPK (14)$\leftrightarrow$E\&M (24)\quad
VPK (18)$\leftrightarrow$E\&M (25)\quad
VPK (19)$\leftrightarrow$E\&M (26)
\end{center}
E\&M Eq.~(23), the differential spectrum, is correct. The term was lost when that
spectrum was integrated analytically in 1968. Their Eq.~(26) and their Table~I
inherit it. Recomputing their Table~I from their own Eq.~(26) reproduces
\textbf{every printed digit}, electrons and muons:

The error has stood for 57 years, and the note is the first place it is corrected.

\subsection*{Where to say this in the NLO paper}

The natural home is \emph{immediately after the closed-form expressions added to
Section~IV}, not in a separate remark and not in the conclusions. The formula
being added \emph{is} E\&M Eq.~(26) with the term restored, so one short
paragraph placed there does three jobs at once: it attributes the expression to
Ref.~[10], it records what had to be corrected, and it explains why the numerical
comparison with Table~I of [10] succeeds only in the small-$r_{cut}$ rows.
Something like:

\begin{quote}
These expressions are Eq.~(26) of Ref.~[10] with the term
$(\alpha/\pi)(3r_{max}/2-r_{max}^{2}/4)$ restored. The differential spectrum,
Eq.~(23) of [10], is correct and agrees with our Eq.~(14); the term was lost in
its analytic integration, so that Eq.~(24), Eq.~(26) and Table~I of [10] are all
low by that amount --- $5\alpha/4\pi=0.0029$ at $r_{max}=1$, and below the quoted
precision for $r_{max}\lesssim0.1$.
\end{quote}

The existing sentence after Table~I, which claims the comparison with [10]
``succeeds'', should then be replaced by a pointer to this paragraph. As it
stands it asserts an agreement that does not hold at the very line it cites
(Section~4 of \texttt{NLO\_comments.pdf}).

Putting the remark anywhere else has drawbacks. In the conclusions it would read
as an isolated swipe at a 1968 paper. As a footnote it would be too easy to miss,
given that everyone using the compact formula has been using the incomplete one.
Next to the formula it is simply part of the provenance.

\section{Table~I of the NLO paper: $m_V=1.5$~GeV is correct}

You suspected it was wrong; it is not. E\&M's table column is $2E$~(MeV), and
$2E=m_V$ because $E=m_V/2$ at resonance (stated below their Eq.~22). The rows are
marked $\rho$ at 765.0, $\omega$ at 782.0, $\phi$ at 1019.5 --- meson masses. So
the last row, 1500.0, is $m_V=1.5$~GeV, exactly what the NLO paper used.
$\delta_R$ settles it, depending on the mass only through $\ln(2E/m)$:
\begin{center}

What \emph{is} wrong is their claim of agreement with that table: at $r_{max}=1$

rows.

\section{New finding: your $k_{max}=M_V/2$ contradicts your own Eq.~(22)}

The text above your Eq.~(16) says the maximum photon energy is $k_{max}=M_V/2$.
That is the massless limit. The exact endpoint is
\begin{equation*}
\omega_{max}=\frac{M_V^{2}-4m_\ell^{2}}{2M_V}=\frac{M_V}{2}-\frac{2m_\ell^{2}}{M_V},
\end{equation*}
which is NLO Eq.~(15) --- \textbf{and it is already in your paper}, as Eq.~(22),
$E_\gamma=(M_{J/\psi}^{2}-M_{ee}^{2})/(2M_{J/\psi})$ evaluated at
$M_{ee}\to2m_\ell$. So Eqs.~(16) and~(22) of your own paper disagree.
No consequence for electrons. For muons $r_{max}$ can only reach $0.99535$, so
the $r_{max}=1$ row of a muon table lies outside phase space.
\textbf{Not fixed --- your call.}
